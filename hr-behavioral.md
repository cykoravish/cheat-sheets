# HR and Behavioral: Interview Cheat Sheet

Here the interviewer is not testing my code. They are testing my **honesty, ownership, communication and attitude**. Short, real and calm answers work best.

**Golden rules:**
- Speak slowly. Use simple words.
- Use **real examples** from my own work. The interviewer will ask follow-up questions, so I only say things I can explain.
- Use the **STAR** pattern for stories: **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult.

---

## 1. "Tell me about yourself."

**What they check:** how clearly I can explain who I am in 1 to 2 minutes.

**Tip:** Do not read my resume line by line. Tell a **short story** with 4 parts: who I am, how I got into coding, what I do now, and what I am good at.

**Template (fill with your own details):**

> "I'm [Name]. Let me share a quick story about myself. Since childhood, I have enjoyed solving puzzles. That curiosity slowly became my passion for debugging code and fixing bugs. After school, I joined [College] and started learning web development. Today I work as a [Role] with [Technologies]. Recently, I built [Project], where I [what I did]. Now I am looking for a chance to grow and take bigger responsibility, and that is why I am here."

Keep it to about 1 minute. End by connecting my story to **this job**.

---

## 2. "Is there any application or website you are proud of?"

**What they check:** if I really understand my own project, and how well I explain my work. They want to see if I know what is written on my resume.

**How to answer:** Pick my **best project** and explain it with STAR.

> "I am proud of [Project]. **The problem** was [what problem]. **My part** was [my role]. I used [tech] because [reason]. **The hard part** was [challenge], and I solved it by [action]. **The result** was [number or outcome, like load time reduced by 40%]."

**Prepare before the interview:** the tech stack and why I chose it, my exact role, the hardest bug, what I would improve now. Be ready for cross-questions, so I do not claim anything I cannot explain.

---

## 3. "What is the biggest problem you have faced and how did you solve it?"

**What they check:** the level of problems I handle, and how I think when I am stuck.

**How to answer:** Pick a **real** problem that was difficult enough to show my skill (a performance issue, a production bug, a tricky deadline). Do not choose something too simple, and do not invent things. If I did not face a big problem, I can say what I would do at that scale, but I will say clearly that it is my approach.

> "In [project], [problem] was happening. I first **found the cause** by [debugging steps]. Then I tried [options] and chose [solution] because [reason]. After the fix, [result]. I also added [test or monitoring] so it does not happen again."

---

## 4. "You found out your teammate made a big mistake. What will you do?"

**What they check:** my honesty and how I handle people.

**Answer:**

> "First, I will **talk to my teammate directly**, in a friendly way, because it may be a small misunderstanding. I will **help them fix it** quickly. If the mistake is serious, or it affects the project or users, or we cannot fix it in time, I will **inform my manager**. I won't hide it, because hiding a problem usually makes it bigger. The goal is to protect the product and the team, not to blame a person."

---

## 5. "You are given a problem above your role or skills. What will you do?"

**What they check:** how I behave when I am in a tough spot, and my attitude to learning.

**Answer:**

> "I will see it as a chance to learn. First I will **understand the problem fully** and break it into small parts. I will check the documentation and study the part I don't know. If I am stuck, I will **ask a senior** for guidance, after I have tried on my own. If the task is critical and there is a deadline, I will **tell my manager early** about the risk, and spend the extra time to learn and finish it."

---

## 6. "You have applied to [Company]. Do you know what we do?"

**What they check:** my interest in the company and how well I prepared.

**Prepare before the interview** (30 minutes is enough):
- What the company does and who their customers are
- Their main product and what technology they use
- Recent news or a new feature
- Why I want to work there

**Answer:**

> "Yes. [Company] builds [product] for [customers]. I have used/seen [product feature] and I liked [specific thing]. I also read that [recent news]. I am interested in the work you do on [area], because it matches my skills in [skill]. If I missed something, I would love to hear more."

A short, specific answer is better than a long general one. It is fine to say "I'm not sure about this part" for something small, but never go in with no preparation.

---

## 7. "You are working on a task and you find another issue. What will you do?"

**What they check:** if I take ownership.

**Answer:**

> "I will first **judge the size** of the new issue. If it is **small** and related to my task, I will fix it along with my current work. If it is **big** and will take a lot of time, I will **not ignore it**. I will report it to my team with details, such as a ticket, and **continue my current task**, so I do not delay my deadline. The team can then decide the priority."

---

## Bonus: other common HR questions

**"What are your strengths and weaknesses?"**
Strength: a real one with an example, such as learning fast or debugging calmly. Weakness: a real but small one that I am **already working on**. For example: "I used to take too long on small details. Now I set a time limit and ask for early feedback."

**"Why do you want to leave your current job?"**
Be positive. Talk about **growth and learning**, not complaints about people. "I have learned a lot here, and now I want to work on [bigger scale / new technology]."

**"Where do you see yourself in 3 to 5 years?"**
"I want to become a strong developer who can own features end to end, and slowly take on more responsibility, such as mentoring juniors."

**"Do you have any questions for us?"**
Always ask 1 or 2. "What does a typical day look like for this team?" and "What would success look like in the first 3 months?"

---

## Last-Minute Revision

- Intro: a short story, not a resume reading.
- Project: STAR. Know my tech stack and my exact role.
- Big problem: a real one, with cause, options, solution and result.
- Teammate's mistake: talk first, help fix, inform the manager if it is serious.
- Above my skills: learn, ask for help, warn early about risks.
- Company question: research 30 minutes before.
- New issue during a task: small means fix it. Big means report it and continue.
- Always ask 1 or 2 questions at the end.
